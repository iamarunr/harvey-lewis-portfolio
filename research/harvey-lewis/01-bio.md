## Narrative Biography

### Origins & Background

Harvey Lewis was born on April 13, 1976 in Wheeling, West Virginia, and grew up in Berea, Ohio, a suburb of Cleveland. He was not a natural athlete in his youth — by his own account he was unhealthy, eating a diet heavy on fast food.

> "Back in high school I ate what was in my culture. My mom was a busy nurse, she didn't have a lot of time so we ate a lot of fast food. Meals were heavier on meat. I was unhealthy overall as a kid, I could grab layers of fat around my stomach, and I always had irregularity using the rest room. I had heartburn, challenges like that."
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([Great Vegan Athletes](https://www.greatveganathletes.com/harvey-lewis-ultramarathon-runner/))

He was intellectually curious even then — exploring the woods of Pennsylvania during childhood, developing a deep affinity for animals that would later inform his ethical choices.

> "I always liked animals a lot. I explored Pennsylvania, I saw all kinds of animals, even saw a black bear occasionally. I loved animals, my cat, my dog, my fish. But I didn't think anything and would just grab something from McDonald's. Something was picking at me."
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([Great Vegan Athletes](https://www.greatveganathletes.com/harvey-lewis-ultramarathon-runner/))

### The Turning Point: Mother's Stroke

When Lewis was 19 (approximately 1995), his mother — a nurse, 54 years old at the time — suffered a stroke. There was no family history. The experience shook him.

> "When I was 19 my mother had a stroke. She was 54 years old, a nurse, and we had no family history. The nutrition was a big element that we could have done differently. It made me rethink everything."
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([Great Vegan Athletes](https://www.greatveganathletes.com/harvey-lewis-ultramarathon-runner/))

Around the same time, he noticed a vegetarian student at his school and announced, on the spot, that he would try vegetarianism too. By 1996 the transformation was underway. That same year — the vegetarian year — he ran his first ultramarathon: the FANS 24-hour Ultra in Minnesota, covering 81.3 miles at age 20 and finishing 24th.

### The Broken Neck

Roughly ten years before his first Badwater win — around 2004 — Lewis suffered a broken neck. Neurosurgeons rebuilt his C4 and C5 vertebrae with titanium implants. Rather than ending his career, the injury and recovery seemed to catalyze something in him.

> "I was fortunate to survive, and after that event I actually have become so much faster. So it's kind of a neat thing; I try to encourage people not to put mental limitations on their abilities."
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([WVXU, July 22, 2014](https://www.wvxu.org/sports/2014-07-22/scpa-teacher-wins-badwater-race))

### The Teacher

Lewis has taught social studies (including government) at the School for Creative and Performing Arts (SCPA) in Cincinnati's Over-the-Rhine neighborhood since 2006. SCPA is a selective public arts magnet school, and Lewis is one of its most distinctive figures — not just for what he teaches, but for how he arrives each morning.

Around 2013–2014, he screened a film about climate change and rising sea levels in his government class, then made a promise to his students: he would stop driving to work. He has kept that promise ever since. Every school day, he runs approximately three miles from his home in East Walnut Hills to SCPA — up Mount Adams for a sunrise view of Eden Park, down to the riverside trail, through downtown's historic cobblestone alleyways, and past Washington Park where he sometimes encounters students before the bell.

> "It's about three miles each way, so it's not a super long distance for me. I just carry my things in my backpack and it's really pretty easy to manage. Oftentimes on my way home, I'll run over to Kentucky and back and add on an extra five miles to that normal route."
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([CityBeat, Nov 2020](https://www.citybeat.com/cincinnati/a-look-inside-the-life-of-cincinnati-ultramarathon-runner-and-educator-harvey-lewis/Slideshow/12233155))

His backpack — described as the size of a mini-fridge, loaded with laptop, clothes, and food — has become an informal symbol of his dual life. On mornings when his students happen to be on the same bus route, he races the bus.

> "That's a great opportunity to show them the benefits of: I'm 47, and … I'm racing a bus? What the hell is going on here?"
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([iRunFar, May 2024](https://www.irunfar.com/the-educator-effect-teachers-in-ultrarunning))

When asked what links his work as a teacher and his life as an ultrarunner, Lewis distilled it to a single conviction:

> "The ultimate common denominator is this desire to always improve ourselves."
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([iRunFar, May 2024](https://www.irunfar.com/the-educator-effect-teachers-in-ultrarunning))

### The Vegan Runner

Lewis became vegetarian in 1996 and made a brief full transition to veganism in 1998 during college — then slipped back to eating dairy when traveling, a compromise he has since said he regrets. In 2016, he recommitted fully to veganism and has not wavered.

> "I've been vegetarian for 25 years and I've been vegan for five years."
>
> — Harvey Lewis, days after 2021 Big Dog's win ([Great Vegan Athletes](https://www.greatveganathletes.com/harvey-lewis-ultramarathon-runner/))

His pre-run breakfast: a bowl of granola with almond milk, topped with chia seeds and dragon fruit. During races: squeezable chia seed fruit pouches, miso soup, coconut water, aloe juice, beans and rice, bean burritos, and whatever vegan ethnic food he can find.

> "Race fuel doesn't make me sick. I eat something big and don't get sick. Other people are eating meat and dairy and it just doesn't process. You can process more calories at a quicker pace with plant foods."
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([Great Vegan Athletes](https://www.greatveganathletes.com/harvey-lewis-ultramarathon-runner/))

His view of veganism extends beyond personal performance. His mother's stroke was the original catalyst, but his perspective has broadened to encompass climate, ethics, and public health:

> "We would do a lot better if we move toward more plant-based foods for the benefit of curbing the No. 1 killer (diseases) in America."
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([CityBeat, Nov 2020](https://www.citybeat.com/cincinnati/a-look-inside-the-life-of-cincinnati-ultramarathon-runner-and-educator-harvey-lewis/Slideshow/12233155))

> "The possibilities that lie out there in 10, 20, 30 years for dealing with things like climate change, health, aging population — this is huge. And it's a cheap fix. This is a very inexpensive way of making a dramatic difference."
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([Great Vegan Athletes](https://www.greatveganathletes.com/harvey-lewis-ultramarathon-runner/))

He credits his plant-based diet directly with his extraordinary recovery capacity. Days after setting the 2021 Big Dog's world record, he ran a local 5-mile race. The day after the record, he ran a mile to continue his running streak.

### Training Philosophy

Lewis's training mileage for Badwater — often 100 miles per week — is conservative by elite ultramarathon standards, where training peaks of 140+ miles per week are common. He has described his approach as trusting the mind over the body.

> "The toughest finish line is the one right in front of you."
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([Rich Roll Podcast ep658, Jan 2022](https://www.richroll.com/podcast/harvey-lewis-658/))

At Backyard Ultra races, he breaks the race into micro-goals — the reward at the end of each loop, whether a small amount of food or two minutes horizontal — rather than focusing on the incomprehensible aggregate.

> "It makes you appreciate what you have in life, because after you finish, a meal never tasted so good."
>
> — Harvey Lewis ([CityBeat, Nov 2020](https://www.citybeat.com/cincinnati/a-look-inside-the-life-of-cincinnati-ultramarathon-runner-and-educator-harvey-lewis/Slideshow/12233155))

He also uses deep breathing (10 breaths to calm the nervous system) and works with sleep consultant Todd Anderson of Dream Recovery — invaluable for the multi-day sleep deprivation of Backyard Ultra racing.

### Activism: Running as Witness

Lewis is remarkable not only for the distances he runs, but for the purpose he has sometimes attached to them.

In 2008, he earned a grant to retrace Mahatma Gandhi's famous 1930 Salt March — 241 miles from Gandhi's Harijan Ashram in Ahmedabad to the coastal city of Dandi. In 2009, he ran Dr. Martin Luther King Jr.'s Selma to Montgomery march (54 miles). Afterward, he met 99-year-old civil rights icon Amelia Boynton Robinson, who told him:

> "I believe God kept me alive 99 years so I can share my story with young people."
>
> — Amelia Boynton Robinson, to Harvey Lewis ([Wikipedia, citing sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Lewis_(ultrarunner)))

Ever the teacher.

### Documentary: *Like Harvey Like Son*

In 2018, Lewis ran the 2,190-mile Appalachian Trail in 49 days and 14 hours, averaging over 42 miles per day and placing in the top 10 for fastest AT completions ever. He collaborated with Harris Media Company throughout the journey to produce the documentary *Like Harvey Like Son*. The film premiered at the Mariemont Theatre in Cincinnati in November 2020 and is available on streaming platforms.

Website: [www.likeharveylikeson.com](https://www.likeharveylikeson.com/)

### Personal Life

Lewis lives in the East Walnut Hills neighborhood of Cincinnati. He has a rescued dog named **Charlie**, who runs long distances with him and eats vegan. He is a friend and running partner of centenarian vegan runner Mike Fremont. He has run every Flying Pig Marathon in Cincinnati since the race's inception in 1999.

He has been to **101 countries across seven continents** — his running life and his traveling life are inseparable.
